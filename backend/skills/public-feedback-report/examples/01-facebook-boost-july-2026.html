TL;DR

Nine posts is a small month, so everything below is ranked rather than given as a percentage, and no trend claim rests on July's volume alone. Seven of the nine came from people who had actually boosted a post. This report is short because the month was: 25 of the 29 channels we swept returned nothing for July. It has not been padded to fill a page count.

July had one subject: money people could see leaving and could not stop. Six of the nine posts are about a charge that could not be cancelled, explained or disputed. Not one is about targeting, reach or results.

1"I can't stop a boost that's already running." Two posts, both Trustpilot, both mid-July, and new this month. A boosted event does not appear in Ads Manager, so it cannot be found or cancelled; the assistant cannot locate, pause or refund it; and Business Suite refuses to edit it. The person watched the spend continue while being unable to stop it. Fix: a cancel that works from where the boost was created. Owner: product. "Their AI can't cancel the event." — Trustpilot, 13 July
2"It said my payment failed, then charged me twice." One post this month, and a second instance of something first seen in June — which is why it is here rather than buried. Payments reported as failing across two cards and Paylode, after which the account showed multiple duplicate payments. Two months running is not yet a trend, but it is the pattern worth watching in August. Owner: engineering. "Now my account shows multiple, duplicate payments made!!!" — Google Play, 1 July
3"There's nobody to reach when it's my money." Three posts — the largest single group in July, and all three belong to Support rather than the product team, so they sit in their own section below. Live chat advertised at 5–10 minutes reported as taking 5–7 hours. An AI assistant that cannot escalate. A hacked-then-disabled account with nothing in the help pages explaining recovery. Every billing failure above ends here, which is why it is worth reading the two together.
4Nobody said they were leaving this month. Zero explicit departures in July. Across everything we hold, three people have ever said so outright — 2014, 2016 and February 2025 — and none named a rival; they stopped rather than switched. We did not count anyone who merely sounded finished, including the reviewer who warned others off boosting but did not say they had stopped.
5Brand new, and from analysts rather than users: the measures changed underneath everyone. From 15 June about 85 long-standing reach and impressions measures stopped updating and were replaced by Views, which counts differently — so any year-on-year comparison breaks at that date. Separately, delivery now reads the ad creative itself to decide who sees it, which puts a single-creative boost at a structural disadvantage. No user has raised either, which most likely means the people affected have not yet worked out what happened.

If you do one thing: make a running boost cancellable from where it was created. It is the only genuinely new user problem this month, it is the most severe, and right now it is being resolved by banks and card disputes instead of by us.

How we compare

No competitor feedback was collected in July, so there is no comparison this month. We are not repeating last period's table as though it were current. Two things still stand and are worth restating in a line each: the destination people name when they stop boosting is Meta Ads Manager, which is ours — so almost nobody here leaves Meta over Boost, they move deeper into Meta. And the two closest outside substitutes, TikTok Promote and LinkedIn Boost, both offer something Boost does not: a business outcome you can buy directly, rather than engagement only. Nobody said they were leaving in July, and no rival was named.

Recommendations

Four items. Two come from July's evidence; two are carried forward because they are still live and were simply quiet this month — they are labelled with the date they were first raised, not presented as new. July did not produce a fifth worth your engineering time, and we have not invented one.
1
Make a running boost cancellable from where it was created
Do first
"I can't stop a boost that's already running."
The month's only new user problem and its most severe. A boosted event does not appear in Ads Manager, so there is no surface on which to stop it; the assistant cannot find, pause or refund it; Business Suite refuses to edit it. The person is left watching spend continue. Evidence: two posts, one source, this month, both from users. Thin by volume, but the failure is specific and reproducible enough to act on without waiting for more.
→ Route to prd-author
2
Trace the failed-payment-then-duplicate-charge path
Do first
"It said my payment failed, then charged me twice."
One post in July and one in June — the same shape both times: repeated rejections across multiple payment methods, then duplicate charges appearing on the account. Meta's help pages explain that boosts accrue and bill later at a threshold, which may account for some of what people are seeing, but a correct explanation in documentation does not stop the bank alert. Worth a trace now, while it is two cases rather than a pattern. Evidence: two posts across two months, both users, both app stores.
→ Route to prd-author
3
Say what the boost is optimising for, on the screen that takes the money
Carried forward
"I paid, and all I got was likes."
Carried forward — first raised February 2014, last seen 11 June 2026, ten posts all time. Quiet in July, not resolved. The flow assigns an engagement goal without saying so, and sales and lead goals are not offered at all. A disclosure change rather than a rebuild: it adds no controls and does not touch the one-decision simplicity that is the button's whole advantage.
→ Route to prd-author
4
Show the reason a boost was blocked, where it was blocked
Carried forward
"It won't let me boost and won't tell me why."
Carried forward — first raised June 2016, last seen January 2026, seven posts all time. Quiet in July. The reasons already exist in Meta's published policies; the failure is that someone stopped mid-purchase has to go and find them. One documented case has a roof-replacement advert blocked under housing policy, which no buyer could have predicted. Cheap, and it is real user pain.
→ Route to prd-author
Watching, not recommending yet. Two analyst-only items from July: the roughly 85 legacy reach measures retired on 15 June, and delivery now reading creative to choose the audience. Both are real and both affect boost, but no user has raised either — so they belong in next month's read, not this month's build list. If August brings user posts about numbers falling, check the June measurement change before diagnosing a performance problem.
